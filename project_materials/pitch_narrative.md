# Spectrum — judge pitch narrative

**A teen should not have to choose between privacy and getting help.**

Today, many digital safety tools are built around watching and blocking. That can miss a human reality: when teens expect punishment or surveillance, they may hide the very conversations where they need support.

Spectrum is a teen-first AI safety companion. A teen chooses a conversation and asks Spectrum to help make sense of it. The model looks for concrete signs of grooming, cyberbullying, coercion, emotional manipulation, or unsafe requests. It points to the words that raised concern, explains the pattern in plain language, and suggests a boundary or next step without blaming the teen.

The trust layer is voluntary. Spectrum can prepare a summary for a parent, counselor, or another trusted adult, but the teen reviews and decides whether to share it. There is no account monitoring and no automatic alert.

We also demonstrate restraint. Our demo includes ordinary friendly conversation alongside grooming and bullying scenarios. A tool that labels everything dangerous will lose trust, so Spectrum is instructed to cite evidence and leave ambiguous situations ambiguous.

Our MVP is a focused Streamlit app using structured LLM output, a teen-centered prompt, synthetic examples, and a short digital-literacy guide. The next step is to test the language and false-positive behavior with youth advisors and child-safety experts, then measure whether teens understand the patterns and feel more able to seek support.

**Spectrum makes AI a conversation starter—not a watcher—and gives the teen control over the next step.**
